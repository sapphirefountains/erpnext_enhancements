# Sapphire Maintenance

A field-service maintenance subsystem. Technicians fill out a **Maintenance Record** on site — a modular visit form instantiated from a **Template** that is *composed of reusable Sections* (chemical dosing, water chemistry, equipment inspection, cleaning tasks). On submit, the record drives downstream ERP automation — Timesheet, Warranty Claim, and a draft Sales Invoice — and is exposed to customers through the portal. Chemicals are recorded, not issued: stock moves by the container on the Stock Scan page (v1.594.0).

Visits are scheduled by the **Maintenance Contract**, the operational counterpart of two existing documents: the signed **Project Contract** (Maintenance Services Agreement — legal terms, service options, access info) and the **Sales Order** (the commercial source per-visit invoices are drawn against). Contracts are built for fast fill-out: a **Service Plan** preset stamps the standard offering in one pick, a batch dialog adds all of a project's water features at once, and the standard seasonal pair (startup/winterization) are two checkboxes.

Technicians fill visits through the **Visit Wizard** (`/desk/visit-wizard`) — a guided, touch-first desk page (steppers, segmented buttons, tap checklists; no grids) that reads and writes the same Maintenance Record, so the desk form stays the supervisor/review surface and all automation is shared.

The module ships its own workspace and `module_def`. Notifications, the print format, and the portal route are installed as fixtures (see [`../hooks.py`](../hooks.py)).

## Data model

```

│                                                step_instructions (Text Editor how-to) shown in the
│                                                Visit Wizard's collapsible per-step panel.
├── Sapphire Section Item (child, istable)        Chemical Dosing | Water Chemistry | Equipment Inspection | Cleaning Tasks
│                                                  one line: label (+item / uom+min+max / options+mandatory by type)
│                                                  + days (v1.619.0): weekdays it is done on, blank = every visit
└── Sapphire Section Image (child, istable)       step_images: how-to photo (Attach Image) + caption

│                                                (safety_instructions/wrapup_instructions Text Editors
│                                                + safety_images/wrapup_images — Sapphire Section Image)

                                                   (wizard shows 📍 line + tap-to-navigate Map link)

Sapphire Service Plan (master)                   one-pick contract preset: default frequency/template,
                                                 visit shape, invoicing cadence, seasonal startup/

                                                 ripple to contracts that already applied it.

Sapphire Maintenance Contract (parent)           operational contract: who/what/when/how billed
│   customer, project, service_plan, default_frequency (materializes onto blank feature rows in
│   validate), project_contract (legal), sales_order (commercial), status, visit_shape (Per Feature |
│   Per Site Visit), default_template, invoicing_frequency. Rates (v1.611.0, copied from the signed

│   the on-site visit: Customer/Project/Contract/Serial No/Technician, clock in/out, paused_duration,
│   visit_date (the day the work was ACTUALLY done — blank means "the day it was submitted"; set

│                                                       custom options from the template)
```

Configuration lives in **ERPNext Enhancements Settings → Maintenance**: fee item, services item group, consumables item group (item-picker filter), default consumables warehouse, and the water-feature Item (Serial No picker filters). **No item codes are hardcoded anywhere in this module** — items travel as Link fields, which survive renames.

## Lifecycle

**Authoring:** compose Sections once, reuse them across Templates. Assign templates per contract feature (falling back to the contract default, then the project/customer Active template).

**Contract fill-out** (built for "3 clicks or less" per input): pick a **Service Plan** — frequency, template, visit shape, invoicing and the seasonal checkboxes stamp in one dropdown; tap **Add Water Features** — the dialog lists the project's serials pre-checked with one shared frequency and first-visit date; per-feature tweaks stay single cell edits. New rows inherit the contract's Visit Frequency and anchor to the Start Date; `validate` backfills whatever arrives blank from any path, so the scheduler never sees a row it can't schedule. A 12-fountain contract is ~10 interactions end to end.

**Scheduling:** the daily job `tasks.predictive_maintenance_scheduling` reads Active Maintenance Contracts — Per Feature shape drafts one record per due feature, Per Site Visit drafts one per site, seasonal visits (flat checkboxes + custom rows, via `iter_seasonal_visits`) draft once a year in their target month. Drafts are bare headers; the form instantiates its tables from the template on first open (`get_visit_payload` — the wizard does this server-side in `get_visit_bootstrap`). Projects without an Active contract fall back to the legacy Sales Order Item cadence fields.

**Switching a contract to Per Site Visit folds its drafts (v1.599.0, [`visit_shape.py`](visit_shape.py)).** The scheduler drafts a site visit only while the contract has no open regular draft, and the per-feature drafts already made for the next trip are exactly that, so the switch alone would not produce a site visit until every one of them was finished as a separate visit. Saving the switch (`on_update`, only when this save changed `visit_shape` to Per Site Visit) folds the next trip's **bare** drafts: dated today or later, never opened (no section rows: an opened draft may have answers still on a phone), nothing on them (answer, note, clock, signature, photo, date chosen, attachment) and still Draft. A folded draft's comments move onto the site visit (v1.599.1), with author, time and thread kept (`db_insert`, so no mention is sent again) and one of each (a training warning by its marker). The scheduler's training warning and the Planner's conflict notes are on almost every draft, and counting them as work left all of Hardware's drafts unfolded the first time. A draft whose morning digest was texted or with an invoice linked is never folded away. The earliest becomes the site visit, converted in place (its `serial_no` cleared with one field write, so no save hook stamps a clock-in), and the others fold into it only when planned the same way (day, technician, crew); their open ToDos are canceled in the same transaction, because `delete_doc` leaves that to a job queued after commit, which a deploy flushes. Overdue drafts stay missed visits, and opened, differently planned or linked drafts are left; each is named in the message. A contract that already has an open site visit folds its per-feature drafts into it when it is bare and upcoming (v1.599.2: a fold left half done, or a switch back and forth), and nothing when somebody has started it. A draft's own Workflow Action is not a link: `delete_doc` clears it in `on_trash` before its own link check, and counting it made every workflow draft look linked (v1.599.2). Labelled drafts are never touched, and switching back to Per Feature leaves an open site visit as it is. Hardware Apartments (four fountains on one invoice) was the reason: Lisa asked how it would work, and Nik chose one visit for all four (2026-10-12).

**Seeing the day (v1.678.0).** A template whose lines name weekdays no longer reads as the checklist every visit gets, and a visit's checklist is built once, for the day it was scheduled or done. So the template form has **Preview a day** (`sapphire_maintenance_template.preview_day`): pick a weekday, see each section's lines with those set to other days struck through. And the **Visit Review** page shows a visit's **Day-specific lines** against the day it was done (else scheduled; `visit_days.review_lines`): the ones on the visit, the ones due that day but missing, and the ones set for other days. A visit done a day late carries its scheduled day's lines, and that is now in front of the reviewer rather than unnoticed. Matched by section and label across water features; never stops the page from opening.

**Once per Site Visit (v1.634.0).** On a Per Site Visit every water feature resolves its own form, and a seasonal visit resolves the contract's seasonal form for all of them, so each feature got the whole list. That is right for 4th West, where each courtyard fountain is drained the same way, and wrong where the form names each fountain's steps itself: Hardware's winterization would have put the courtyard heaters and the East Wall drain on all seven tabs, lobbies included, and Sanctuary's changeovers on thirteen. A template ticked **Once per Site Visit** (`site_wide`) is instantiated once on a site visit, its rows untagged (`get_visit_payload`). The wizard shows untagged rows on a **Whole site** tab, after the features' tabs, when tagged rows sit beside them (`VZ_SITE`, `feature_serial`), and with no tabs when every row is untagged. Since v1.635.0 a step change never opens on a tab with nothing to do there (`tab_with_rows`); only a template's untagged rows (they carry a `section`) add the tab to a one-feature site visit; and a chemical added in the wizard keeps the feature tab it was added on (`serial_no` is now wizard-writable on consumables, and `follow_ups.check_consumable_features` clears one the visit does not cover). The review email and the portal label untagged rows Whole site; the customer report lists the covered features only. A Per Feature visit is unaffected.

**Winter pause** (`pause_over_winter` on the contract, v1.574.0) is for fountains that are drained for winter. When a regular visit is submitted, the next date normally moves forward one interval. On a paused contract, a next date that lands between the 1st of the Winterization Month and the Startup Month (the window wraps the year end) moves to the 1st of the Startup Month. That is the day the Spring Startup visit is drafted, and regular visits restart then too. Submitting the Winterization visit moves every feature on the contract there. The window starts *at* the winterization month on purpose, because the winterization visit replaces that month's regular visit and a weekly site has to stop at its last early-autumn visit. A next visit date typed onto the contract by hand is never deferred. `defer_for_winter` in `api/maintenance_scheduling.py` holds the rule, and `tests/test_maintenance_winter_pause.py` runs it bench-free. Leave it unticked for fountains that keep running with a heater.

**Visit Wizard** (`/desk/visit-wizard/<record>[/<step>[/<serial>]]`, `?record=…` still accepted, or no record for the picker): Safety briefing + PPE acknowledge gate → Water Chemistry (numeric cards with range chips; out-of-range turns red with the server's verdict on autosave) → Cleaning (tap for done, **N/A** for a task this visit does not need, and a note on each; v1.598.0) → Inspection (segmented buttons honoring custom per-question options; Fail/Replace reveal notes + photo) → Chemicals Used (pre-listed from the template; `[−] qty [+]` steppers stepping by `qty_step`, first tap jumps to `default_qty`; ad-hoc item add) → Wrap-up (**Tasks not done**: each cleaning task left not done with nothing said, on every water feature, asks why; notes + dictation, the fountain photo, signature pad, workflow-aware Finish, which asks once before finishing with a task still unexplained but never blocks). **Steps are one per row type, and `build_steps` orders them the way a visit is worked:** Water Chemistry always comes first — pH and ORP are read on arrival, before cleaning or dosing moves them — and Chemicals Used always comes last, once nothing more will be added. Cleaning and Inspection sit between them in the order the template lists their first sections: `_section_meta` (`api/maintenance_visit.py`) sends each section's `order` in the record's template for this. So a site's wrap-up checks ("Chemicals used?") follow its cleaning list, and a template that inspects before it drains (Tiered Garden winterization) still does. With no template positions, Cleaning goes first (v1.573.0 — before that, the order was fixed as Chemistry → Chemicals Used → Inspection → Cleaning, so a technician counted chemicals and answered "Chemicals used?" before doing the work). The desk form, the portal view and the printed report list the tables Chemistry → Cleaning → Checklist → Consumables. Within a step, rows follow the template's section order, so templates list their pH & ORP section ahead of any other Water Chemistry section. Per Site Visit records get sticky per-feature tabs. The tab strip keeps its scroll position when a step, a tab or a save draws it again, and after a reload (v1.712.0, `restore_tabs_scroll` / `remember_tabs_scroll`, one visit at a time in this phone's `localStorage` under `vz-tabs-scroll`). It then moves only as far as it takes to show the open tab. Before this it came back scrolled to the start, so on Sanctuary's 13 water features the technician scrolled back to their fountain at every step. Steps autosave with optimistic locking (`api/maintenance_visit.py`); a stale desk-side edit rejects the write instead of losing it.

**Back and Forward.** Every screen — the picker, each step, each feature tab — is its own browser history entry, so the phone's Back goes back one screen and Forward restores it; Back from the first screen leaves the page (from the kiosk, which opens the wizard in a new tab, that closes the tab). **Kept on the phone until the server has it (v1.596.0, TASK-2026-02483).** Before this, an answer not yet saved lived only in the page's memory. A reload, a crash, or the phone closing the browser at a fountain with no signal lost it. Nik's decision of 2026-10-12 was "never lose answers", without a full offline mode: the visit is still opened with signal, at the truck. From then on, nothing entered is lost.

- **Answers go to `localStorage` on every change** (`vz-unsent:<visit>`). What is kept is everything the server does not have yet (`VisitWizard.unsent`): edits on screen, a save still on the wire, saves parked against a visit left behind, and consumables added here. It is rewritten when a save lands and removed when nothing is left.
- **Photos, and a Finish with its drawn signature, go to IndexedDB** (`vz-outbox`). They are too big for `localStorage`, and a Finish has to outlive the tab.
  - A photo is kept first and uploaded second. With no signal it waits, and its camera button turns amber.
  - A Finish with no signal shows "Saved on this phone". The visit, its photos and the Finish are then sent in that order.
  - They go when signal returns: the browser's `online` event, any save that gets through, the page opening again, and a backoff while anything waits (15 s, doubling to 2 minutes).
- **Opening a visit puts back what the phone kept.** Each kept answer remembers the server's value from before it was changed (`base`), and the server's version now decides (`vz_reconcile`):
  - unchanged on the server: the phone's answer goes back on;
  - already there (a save that landed but lost its answer): it is dropped;
  - changed by someone else: the technician is asked, on a screen of its own, which to keep.
  - A newer server edit is never overwritten without saying so. A save that lands while the same answer was changed again moves that answer's before-value along (`rebase`), so the second change is not mistaken for a clash later.
  - A question left unanswered (Back, a closed tab) stays a question. It is kept apart (`held`) and asked again on every open, whatever the version, and is never sent in the background.
  - A consumable added on the phone is taken for a server row only if the phone did not already know that row by name (`known`), and only on no water feature or the same one.
- **Visits whose answers, photos or Finish are still on the phone** head the list ("Saved on this phone, not sent yet"). They are sent in the background (`sync_stored`) through the read-only `api/maintenance_visit.get_visit_state`, so opening them is not needed. `get_visit_bootstrap` would claim the visit and instantiate its template on every retry.
  - **Final versus try-later (`vz_try_later`).** Only Frappe turning a request down (an exception it raised, a 4xx) stops a visit's sending for good, and is said. These are sent again later, like no signal: no answer at all, an expired session (401), throttling (408, 429), a gateway's 502/503/504 while the bench restarts on a deploy, and a server error with no Frappe exception in its body. A refused save is kept and named. A visit that can no longer be read is not retried, and an answer without the visit in it is never read as "submitted".
  - **A Finish that is refused, or held back by a clash or a refused save,** does not keep the visit on "Saved on this phone". The visit opens and says why, and the technician finishes it again. A Finish held for a clash goes with the technician's choice. One that already went through (its answer lost) is recognized from the visit's state and not sent twice. A 500 on Finish is shown, not kept as if there were no signal.
  - A visit waiting on the technician offers **Discard what this phone kept**, after a confirmation, once any send of it still on the wire has answered. A photo the server refused is never deleted unseen: opening its visit tries it again, and turned down again it stays kept and marked, to retake or discard.
- **Each photo has its own key** (a strictly increasing time), so a retake while the first is still uploading is the one the answer keeps, and a retake replaces an earlier one still waiting. Photos upload oldest first, one run per visit, and the background sender leaves a visit alone while its run is out.
- **Known limit:** two tabs on one phone editing the same visit both write its kept copy, and the last write wins. Nothing is lost while both stay open.
- **The save state says "Saved on this phone - will send when signal returns"** when there is no signal and the answers are kept. "NOT SAVED - retrying" now means the phone could not keep them, or the server refused them. The close-tab warning is shown only where the browser will not keep answers.
- **Storage that is blocked or full** (a private window, site data turned off) leaves the wizard working as it did before.
- `scripts/test_wizard_back_forward.mjs` ("Visit Wizard: kept on the phone") runs the real page with a phone's storage that outlives a reload. It proves:
  - answers survive a reload with no signal and arrive once;
  - a photo and a Finish queued offline each go out exactly once;
  - a lost-answer save or Finish is not sent twice;
  - an office edit is asked about, never overwritten, also when the question was left unanswered;
  - each failure mode two independent reviews found has a test that fails without its fix (21 tests in the suite), including a deploy's 503 on a photo and on Finish.

**On submit**, `SapphireMaintenanceRecord.on_submit` enqueues [`api/maintenance_workflow.py::process_maintenance_submission`](../api/README.md) (background, "default" queue) which runs isolated steps:

- `create_timesheet` — labour = `clock_out − clock_in − paused_duration`; writes `total_labor_cost`. That field is at **permlevel 1**, readable by System Manager only (v1.542.0), because with the clock times it gives away the technician's burdened pay rate; it is written by `db_set`, which bypasses permlevel, and it is stripped with `apply_fieldlevel_read_permissions()` from the Visit Wizard bootstrap (`get_visit_bootstrap`) and from the `maintenance_visit_history` assistant tool, since `as_dict()` and `get_doc` apply no field-level permissions of their own.
- `check_warranty_and_rma` — Fail/Replace rows grouped per in-warranty feature → one native **Warranty Claim** each + `warranty_rma_flag`.
- `create_sales_invoice` — only when the contract bills **Per Visit**; draft SI from the fee item/services group (Settings) + consumed consumables, against the contract's Sales Order. The base line bills at the contract's `visit_rate` when it is set (v1.611.0), ahead of the Sales Order line's rate or the price list.
- `log_out_of_range_readings` — timeline Comment listing flagged readings.

Next-visit dates roll forward via the `on_submit` doc-event [`api/maintenance_scheduling.py::update_next_visit_dates`](../api/README.md): contract feature rows are the source of truth, with the dates mirrored to the Sales Order Item custom fields for legacy reports.

## Maintenance Planner

A month or week calendar of every maintenance visit (`/app/maintenance-planner`, v1.576.0). People move visits by dragging their cards to another day. On a phone or tablet the finger has to rest on a card for a moment before it lifts, so a swipe still scrolls. Clicking a card opens it, and its dialog changes the date or the technician without dragging. Each month, week and view is its own route, so Back and Forward step through them. The page is in [`page/maintenance_planner/`](page/maintenance_planner/) and its backend in [`api/maintenance_planner.py`](../api/maintenance_planner.py).

**Its Technician filter is in the address** (custom pages S5c, v1.676.0, TASK-2026-02807): `?technician=<user>`, or `__none__` for Unassigned, on every planner screen, e.g. `/desk/maintenance-planner/week/2026-10-05?technician=<user>`. A link with it opens the planner on that technician and a reload keeps it. It is read from `frappe.route_options` on every landing and consumed (`take_route_filters`), after the last-used view is restored, so the address wins; an address without it keeps the technician on screen and gets it written in. What an address gave is that visit's only: not remembered, and "last used" keeps the person's own technician while it is on screen. A bare address after it brings back the person's own, but the planner's own next month or week does not count as a bare address. A technician the planner does not list, or one that is the person's own anyway, stops being the link's. Picking a technician (`pick_filter`) rewrites the entry on screen in place (`write_filter_url`, the page's one history write, never a push: a filter is not a screen) and is remembered as before; a month, week or view is a route carrying it (`filter_options`), so Back and Forward walk the screens, each with the technician it had. A technician the loaded planner does not list becomes All, and the address follows. The two boxes beside it, Projected visits and Project work, are display switches and stay remembered only. The rules are the Project Planner's, explained in full in [its README](../project_enhancements/README.md#project-planner-v15770); guarded by `tests/test_planner_url_filters.py`.

**Its stylesheet** (custom pages S3.4e, v1.673.0) is `page/maintenance_planner/maintenance_planner.css`, which Frappe's page loader sends with the page; the script writes no `<style>` element. The file is on the app's `--sf-*` tokens ([docs/app-design-system.md](../../docs/app-design-system.md)), so it follows the Desk's light or dark theme, and keeps one section per phase in the order the script used to inject them. A page's CSS stays in `<head>` for the session, so every selector starts with an `mp-` class. A card's left edge says what kind of visit it is: a scheduled visit (and a contract's next one) is on the Service pillar's edge, a seasonal visit the warn mix, one pending review the warn fill on a warn tint, an extra visit violet, a done one green, an overdue one red, a projected one a dashed muted edge; the read-only bookings take their pillar's edge (a project task Build, a rental Rent, travel Neutral). Two colors are data rather than design, a technician's and Color by's: the script hands them over as `style="--mp-color: ..."` and `--mp-p6c-c`, and the CSS paints with them. Both legends draw the cards' own classes, and the printed page reads the palette tokens behind the edges. planner_kit's shared `pk-` rules are `public/css/planner_kit.bundle.css`, loaded with the kit (`MP6A.kit`) after this file. `tests/test_s3_page_css.py` holds all of it.

**Fountain Move jobs are scheduled here too** (Nik, 2026-10-21; `project_enhancements/fountain_move_jobs.py`). A move job is a Service project with *Fountain Move* ticked. Each of its open tasks with hours (the template's *Move day*; the office tasks have 0 h) is a card of its own (`kind: "move"`, a *Move* chip, `moves` and `unscheduled_moves` in `get_planner`): drag it to a day, onto another person's row in the crew view, or drop a technician on it, and `move_fountain_move` saves it through the Project Planner's own write, conflicts and reason included. Undo puts the day and crew back; Draft mode refuses them (drafts are visits). The same task is read-only on the Project Planner, which is the deliberate exception to "each planner moves only its own work", and its read-only *Project* booking is left off this page so it is not shown twice. The hours still count in each person's day through the shared engine.

There are three kinds of card:

- **Visit records.** An open draft sits on its Scheduled Visit Date; a visit pending review or submitted sits on the day the work was done. Dragging a draft rewrites `scheduled_visit_date` by saving the record under the mover's own permissions, so the workflow and both validate warnings (time off, training) run. Those warnings come back as orange alerts beside the move, not as a dialog. A draft with a `visit_date` has been started (a Log a Visit, a backfill), so it does not move. Neither do finished visits. A technician change moves the dispatch assignment with it.
- **Projected visits** (dashed). The nightly scheduler only drafts a visit seven days ahead (`tasks.MAINTENANCE_DRAFT_HORIZON_DAYS`), so without these the calendar would go blank after a week. They are worked out from each Active contract's feature rows: `next_visit_date`, then `frequency`, through the same winter pause as the roll-forward. A Per Site Visit contract comes round at its most frequent feature, because one visit rolls every feature from the same day. An open regular draft is the next visit, and the series continues from it. The first dashed card of a series (blue edge, "Next visit") is the contract's stored next visit date, and dragging it rewrites that date on the feature rows the scheduler will read. On a site visit, that is every row due on that day plus any due before the new day. The dashed cards after it are arithmetic: they follow whenever the visit before them is actually done, so they do not move. Moving a projected visit into the seven-day window drafts it at once on the exact day dropped (`_draft_maintenance_record(..., exact_date=True)`); the next nightly run would otherwise book a visit dropped on today for tomorrow. Moving a projected visit needs write on the contract (Projects Manager).
- **Seasonal visits** not drafted yet, on the 1st of their month, the day the scheduler drafts them. They are not movable until they are drafted.

## Visit Review

Phase A of the 2026-10-12 Maintenance tools plan (TASK-2026-02473, v1.595.0) is for the four
people who review every visit before it is billed: Lisa, James, Nik and Clegg, who hold
**Maintenance Reviewer**.

**The page** (`/desk/visit-review`, [`page/visit_review/`](page/visit_review/), backend
[`api/maintenance_review.py`](../api/maintenance_review.py)) has two screens, each its own
history entry.

- **The queue** lists every visit in Pending Review, longest waiting first.
  - Waiting is counted from when the visit last went for review: the workflow's own "Pending
    Review" timeline comment, else the visit's last change.
  - Each row shows the site, visit date, technician and crew, days waiting, how much is filled
    in, out-of-range readings and blank required items by name, the photo count, and "First
    recorded visit".
- **`/desk/visit-review/<visit>`** shows every answer, with **Approve & Submit**, **Send back…**,
  Previous/Next, and **Open the form**. After a decision the page moves to the next visit
  waiting, replacing the decided visit's history entry.

How the page behaves:

- **Its answers are the review email's.** It shows `review_digest.build_context`, the context the
  review email is rendered from, so the two cannot disagree. The email's button now opens the
  visit here.
- **Both decisions go through `apply_workflow` as the reviewer.**
  - The transitions are found by where they lead (to a submitted state, or back to the first
    state), not by their labels.
  - The workflow's roles and its self-approval rule hold as on the form. Whoever started
    a visit is offered no Approve and is told another reviewer approves it.
  - **Nobody approves their own visit on this page.** The workflow's rule looks only at who
    created the record, and the nightly scheduler creates most visits (owner Administrator). So a
    reviewer who is the visit's technician or on its crew is offered no Approve either, and the
    endpoint refuses them. The desk form still applies only the workflow's own rule.
  - A Maintenance Supervisor who is not a reviewer sees the queue and decides nothing.
- **A decision on a changed visit is refused.** It must carry the `modified` the page showed, so
  a visit changed since then (the technician still editing, another reviewer) gets a reload.
- **Every show reloads**, so Back from the form never shows a decision another reviewer has
  since made. A warning the decision's save raises (training, who is going) stays on screen
  until it is closed; only then does the page move on.
- **Send Back needs a note.** It goes on the timeline and to the technician in a "Sent back to
  you" email (`review_digest.send_sent_back`). The email holds the note, then every answer, with a
  **Fix the visit** button into the Visit Wizard.

**Missed and not sent** (A2, [`missed_visits.py`](missed_visits.py)) is the bottom of the queue
screen and the **Missed Maintenance Visits** report. **Nothing is sent automatically** (Nik,
2026-10-12). It lists three kinds of row:

- **Not started**: an open draft dated before today with nothing filled in.
- **Started, not sent**: an open draft dated before today with answers.
- **Not drafted**: a contract visit that fell due with nothing drafted for it.
  - It uses the scheduler's own dedupe rule: an open *regular* draft for the fountain, or for the
    contract on a Per Site Visit contract, which gets one row.
  - It skips a customer on service hold and a contract not started yet, as the scheduler does.
  - It also leaves out a date inside a paused contract's winter window, which the scheduler
    would draft: the fountain is drained (Nik's plan: "a paused contract shows nothing"). The
    roll-forward already defers such dates, so only a date typed by hand can be there.
  - It also covers seasonal visits whose month went by this year with no stamp and no record,
    unless the contract started after that month was over.

A draft's date is its `visit_date`, else its Scheduled Visit Date. Today's visits, visits in
review and unscheduled drafts are not late. The Open link goes to the Visit Wizard, where the
day the work was done can be set (v1.592.0), or to the contract for a visit never drafted.

**Compared with last visit** (A3, [`visit_compare.py`](visit_compare.py)) puts each water reading
beside its value at the site's previous approved visit, in one helper read by three surfaces:

- the review email: "7.4 (was 7.9)" inside the Value cell, keeping the four-column table;
- the Visit Review page, through the same context;
- a "Compared with last visit" panel on the desk form's dashboard
  (`get_dashboard_context(record=...)`).

The rules:

- Only approved visits count, since one still in review may be sent back.
- The previous visit is dated on or before this one, so a backfilled visit compares with what
  came before it. A visit's date is `visit_date`, else the Scheduled Visit Date, else the day it
  was created.
- Readings match on their label, ignoring case and spacing, and on the same water feature: the
  row's tag, else the record's. A contract that changed between Per Feature and Per Site Visit
  still lines up.
- A reading the last visit did not take is looked for further back, through up to `LOOKBACK`
  (10) approved visits *to that water feature*: its own records, or site visits. Counted per
  site, a twelve-fountain Per Feature site's last ten records would all be other fountains'.
- The earlier visits are read in SQL by the day the work was done, so an older visit's
  dashboard is not crowded out by the visits approved after it.

With no approved visit to the site before this one, every surface says "First recorded visit".

A visit with **no** `visit_date` is still refused: it is a blank scheduler draft, which the move would date today and which, approved, would roll its contract forward and make stock, time and invoice entries for a visit that never happened (thirteen were bulk-sent on 2026-10-13 and correctly refused).
- **Client sign-off by email (v1.614.0).** Nik, 2026-10-13: "will this signature requirement allow the tech to send it via email to the client to sign off? the clients aren't always available to sign in person". On Wrap-up, under the client's pad, *Client not here? Email them a link to sign off* takes an address, prefilled from the last address this client was sent a link at, else the agreement's contact (Project Contract `contact_email`), else the account's email or primary contact. Finish sends it with the visit (`finish_visit`'s `email_sign_off`); the server emails the link **after** the visit has moved on, in the same transaction and inside a savepoint, so a bad address or a failed send comes back as a message beside a visit that went for review anyway, and a Finish kept on the phone without signal carries the address and sends it once when it goes. A signature drawn on the pad wins and nothing is emailed. The visit form shows *Client sign-off: …* and **Email Client to Sign Off** / **Re-send Sign-off Link** to the technician, the crew, Maintenance Reviewer / Supervisor, Projects Manager and System Manager (`sign_off.can_send`; write permission alone is not enough, since every Maintenance User has it), and only once the visit has left Draft: Pending Review or submitted (`sign_off.finished`), since the client confirms the service was performed. The link opens `/visit-sign-off`: the service report, a typed name, a pad and "I confirm the service described above was performed". Signing writes four fields (`client_sign_off`, `client_signed_by`, `client_signed_on`, `client_sign_off_method` = "By email") with `db_set`, so an approved visit takes it without its submit pipeline running again, and a form left open since cannot save over it. They are deliberately **not** `allow_on_submit` (`db_set` does not need it, and a save after submit skips `validate`, so it would have let anyone with write forge or inject a signature) and are `no_copy`. The typed name loses control characters and `<` / `>` before it is stored. A pad signature is stamped "On site" and the time in `validate`. The link is a bearer token (256 bits; only its SHA-256 is stored on the **Visit Sign-Off Request**), expires after 14 days, signs once, and a resend supersedes it; unknown, malformed, expired, superseded and canceled links get the same words from the endpoint. **The client's sign-off never blocks review, approval or billing.** The report prints "Signed on site <time>", "Signed by email by <name> on <time>" or "Sent for sign-off <date>, not yet signed"; the review digest says the same.
- **Portal:** route `/maintenance-records` is registered for the **Customer** role (`portal_menu_items` in `hooks.py`); `get_context` sets `show_labor` from the Sales Order's `custom_display_labor_hours`.

## Gotchas

- **Never write the wizard's address by hand.** It once wrote `/app/visit-wizard?record=…` with `history.replaceState`; the v16 router strips only `desk/`, so Back or Forward onto that entry parsed as a Page called `app` and showed "Page not found" (a reload worked, via frappe's server-side `/app` → `/desk` redirect, which is why it survived). Route with `frappe.set_route` segments. The record form's **Open Visit Wizard** does (`frappe.set_route("visit-wizard", frm.doc.name)`, v1.619.1); it set `window.location` to `/app/visit-wizard?record=…`, a full page reload that Back reloaded again. The kiosk's `/app/visit-wizard?record=` links still work: they open a new tab, a full load the server redirects.
- A chemistry reading of **0 means "not measured"** — Float fields can't distinguish blank from zero, so zero values are never flagged out-of-range; a true zero reading belongs in the row's notes.
- Reading-range overrides on Serial No match section items **by label string**; renaming a reading label silently orphans its overrides.
- The historical-visits panel is an **HTML field** rendered from `get_historical_visits` — not a stored child table.
- Only one **Active** contract per project (validated); the scheduler auto-expires contracts past their `end_date`, or auto-renews a fixed-term one (`api/maintenance_renewal.py`), raising `visit_rate`, `recurring_amount` and both emergency rates by `renewal_increase_percent`, compounded per term, with a comment and notices to Projects Manager and Accounts Manager (v1.611.0). A contract that existed before v1.611.0 reads 0 and renews at the same rates: it was agreed under the old "then-current rates" wording. A new contract takes its signed agreement's figure (12% by default there) and starts at 0 without one. Month-to-month contracts do not renew, so §9.3's anniversary increase is applied by hand.
- **Billing seasons (v1.625.0).** A Monthly contract with **Bill From** / **Bill Through** months (`billing_season_start` / `billing_season_end`) is billed only in season by the recurring-billing job (`api/maintenance_billing.py`). A month bills its base fee when its billing period's middle day is in those months and on or after the Start Date (`period_is_billable`): five invoices for "May 1st through September 30th, billed monthly" whatever day of the month the contract bills on, and never the end of a season before the agreement starts. Outside the season the chemicals still bill, on a draft of their own; a period with nothing to charge drafts nothing. An Annual Fee is split over the season's months, not twelve. Blank both months to bill all year; a Monthly contract refuses one without the other. Seasons are Monthly only (a quarter bills whole or not at all), and independent of the winter pause, which moves visits, not bills. Valley Fair Mall and Village at Day Dairy bill May to September.
- A **Service Plan is a stamp, not a live link** — editing a plan later does *not* update contracts that already applied it. Re-pick the plan on a contract to re-stamp.
- Feature-row **frequency is materialized** (visible on the row, the scheduler reads it directly). Changing the contract's Visit Frequency offers "apply to all rows"; there is no hidden inheritance at runtime.
- The seasonal visit labels **"Seasonal Startup"** and **"Winterization"** are load-bearing strings (draft dedup + cadence-skip key on them) — constants in the contract controller, never reword.
- **`visit_label` is a cadence switch, not a caption.** `update_next_visit_dates` returns early on *any* truthy label, so giving a record a label — for display, for grouping, for anything — silently stops it advancing the schedule. That is deliberate for seasonal visits and for `create_visit_today`'s "Extra Visit" pull-forward, and it is why `create_visit` (unscheduled / backfilled) leaves the label empty. If you ever want a labelled visit that *does* advance the cadence, the early return is what you have to change.
- **An empty `covered_features` table breaks a contract in two places at once, and both fail silently.** The scheduler builds `due_features` only from that table (so a Per Site Visit contract drafts nothing, forever), and `get_visit_payload` loops it to emit rows (so a record opened by hand instantiates an *empty form*). The contract looks perfectly healthy — Active, template set, frequency set. A feature row needs a **Serial No**, so the real prerequisite is one Serial No per water feature with `custom_project` set and `item_code` matching Settings → `water_feature_item`.
- **A `default` on `visit_date` would be actively wrong.** It is a normal doctype, so MariaDB writes a new column's default into *every existing row* — stamping a service date onto historical records that never had one, and feeding it to the cadence roll-forward. Blank must keep meaning "unknown, use the submission date". (See the CLAUDE.md gotcha; Singles behave the opposite way.)
