# Travel Management

Crew-based trip planning, logistics and travel finance. A non-submittable **Travel Trip** hub document carries a crew of travelers, the booking segments (flights / lodging / ground / misc costs / personal-vehicle mileage), a day-by-day agenda with CRM outcome capture, and rolls everything into native **Expense Claims**, **Employee Advances** and **Vehicle Logs**. Redesigned ground-up in v1.15.0 (the old submittable + Workflow version is retired; its 2 production drafts were deleted by patch).

Trips are entered on **Plan a Trip** (`/desk/plan-a-trip`, v1.520.0): a step-by-step desk page for the office booking a crew — the trip, who's going, getting there, getting back, where everyone sleeps, getting around, freight, the schedule, the files — ending on a **trip checklist** (a bed every night, travel both ways, confirmation and tracking numbers, cost — and, as a separate, quieter tally, the paperwork for every booking that is made). Location boxes (drive from/to, freight ship-from/deliver-to, a stop's Place) search Google for place names and addresses through the same component as the Address form. The Travel workspace tile and the list's *+ Add* button open it; the form links to it and shows the checklist as a headline. The form is still the full record.

A saved trip can also be looked at whole, from any step of Plan a Trip or from the form's **Trip views** buttons (v1.546.0): an **Overview** (every day from start to end, each booking once with a chip per person carrying that person's own confirmation number, and what the checklist says is missing shown on the day or booking it is about), a **Crew grid** (people down the side, days across, an icon per flight, night in a room, ride, stop and shipment), **Side by side** (a column per person, a row per day) and **View as** (exactly what one person's `/itinerary` shows, plus their itinerary email and calendar invite rendered without sending). See [Trip views and the money rule](#trip-views-and-the-money-rule).

A trip keeps its **files** (v1.547.0, PR 2 of Nik's 2026-09-26 program): every flight, room, ride and shipment card on Plan a Trip has *Attach a file* for its paperwork — a boarding pass, the hotel's confirmation, the rental agreement, a bill of lading — for everyone on the booking or for one person (each person's own boarding pass), and the **Files** step (between Schedule and Review) holds the files for the whole trip that belong to no booking — a site map, the safety plan, an insurance certificate, the job packet — for the whole crew or for one person. Each is a **Trip Document** row. On `/itinerary` each booking card lists its files as big tap targets (a boarding pass is shown at the gate), a picture opens in a viewer inside the page and a PDF in the phone's own viewer, and the **Documents** screen lists every file that person can see. See [Trip files](#trip-files).

## Design decisions (the "why")

- **No Workflow, no submit.** Trips are edited *collaboratively* (admins and travelers alike) until Closed; a submittable doc would freeze mid-trip edits behind amend cycles. Lifecycle is a plain `status` Select: **Planning → Booked → In Progress → Completed → Closed**. In Progress/Completed auto-advance from the trip dates (daily job); Booked and Closed are manual. Closed locks the doc (controller check); `api.reopen_trip` is the coordinator escape hatch.
- **The parent has NO `employee` field — and must never get one.** Travelers live in the `travelers` child table; the Employee dashboard's Travel Trip count works because frappe's link-count filter falls back to the `Trip Traveler.employee` child column. A parent field named `employee` would silently zero that dashboard count.
- **Documents are created explicitly, never as save side-effects** — `travel_management/api.py` methods back the form's Create buttons. Claim dedupe is stamp-based (see below) so re-running is always safe.
- **Multi-currency is out of scope for v1**: all costs are company-currency; advances are created at exchange rate 1. Foreign receipts get entered converted.
- **One row per person, one card per booking.** Plan a Trip shows a flight with four people on it as one card; it stores four Trip Flight rows, each pinned to its traveler with that person's own confirmation number, sharing a hidden `booking_group`. That is what lets each traveler's `/itinerary`, itinerary email and calendar invite show their own booking and nobody else's (a pinned row is hidden from everyone else — the existing visibility rule). Rooms and vehicles work the same way; a room's or vehicle's total cost is split evenly across its rows. `planner.py`'s docstring has the write rules: a shared field is only copied across a booking's rows when the page changed it, the split is only redone when the total, the people or the guests changed, and a row on an Expense Claim is never deleted or re-priced.
- **A room can have guests** (v1.544.0). Someone sharing another person's room at no cost (an upgraded room, a spare bed) is ticked into that room like anyone else, so the hotel and its confirmation are on their itinerary, then marked *staying free* (Trip Accommodation `guest`). A guest's row gets no share of the cost, sorts after the people paying for the room (everything that reads "a booking's dates" reads its first row), and has its check-in/out fitted to the guest's own nights (`planner.fit_guest_stays`), so their calendar invite doesn't have them checking in before they've flown out.
- **The checklist flags, it never blocks.** The office asked to *see* what is missing, so `completeness.py` feeds the page and a form headline, and nothing refuses a save or a status change. It returns data, not sentences; the page words it. Its fifth check, paperwork, is not even counted with the other four: it is a separate, quieter tally of files not attached yet (v1.547.0; see [Trip files](#trip-files)).
- **The checklist reads what was booked, not only the dates** (v1.544.0). A bed is needed only on the nights someone is away: their own dates, *narrowed* by their own flights and drives (`completeness.stay_window`), so a day trip needs no room whatever dates the crew step has. And a round-trip ticket is one charge, so the flight home carries no cost of its own: a no-cost flight whose confirmation number (PNR) is on a flight that has a cost is not flagged (`completeness.on_another_ticket`). Splitting the fare across both flights would give an Expense Claim two lines for one charge. Flights only: two rooms on one hotel confirmation are still two charges.
- **A receipt is money; a booking's paperwork is not** (v1.547.0). Each cost row's own Attach field (fieldname `attachment`) is now labeled **Receipt** and sits in the row's cost section, on Trip Flight, Trip Accommodation, Trip Ground Transport and Trip Freight (freight's was "BOL / Paperwork"; Trip Expense already said Receipt). A receipt says what was paid, so under the money rule it stays with its cost, which the views never show, and `shape_itinerary` stopped sending `attachment` at all. What the crew needs to *carry* — a boarding pass, a confirmation, a rental agreement, a bill of lading — is a Trip Document, which everyone on the trip can open. Prod held no attachment on any travel row when this changed (checked 2026-09-26), so no patch moves anything.

## Data model

```
Travel Trip (parent, NOT submittable, autoname TRIP-.YYYY.-.#####)
│   status Planning/Booked/In Progress/Completed/Closed; booked_on/closed_on stamps
│   travel_for_doctype/travel_for_name  → Project | Opportunity | Lead | Customer
│   project (read-only mirror when travel_for is a Project) · customer (derived)
│   billable (default for new cost rows) · 8 read-only financial rollups
├── travelers        → Trip Traveler         (employee, own from/to dates, per-diem calc,
│                                             expense_claim/advance back-links + statuses,
│                                             reminder/nudge idempotency stamps)
├── flights          → Trip Flight           (airline → Supplier, PNR, cost block)
├── accommodations   → Trip Accommodation    (hotel → Supplier, confirmation, cost block,
│                                             guest = staying free in someone else's room)
├── ground_transport → Trip Ground Transport (typed links: supplier OR vehicle+vehicle_log;
│                                             Company Fleet rows forced company-paid)
├── freight          → Trip Freight          (carrier → Supplier, tracking/PRO/BOL #, pickup +
│                                             delivery windows, received-by traveler, cost block,
│                                             hidden booking_group = the shipment's id)
├── other_costs      → Trip Expense          (misc: parking, tolls, fees; cost block)
├── mileage          → Trip Mileage          (personal vehicle only: distance × settings rate)
├── itinerary        → Trip Agenda           (related party dyn-link, location → Travel POI,
│                                             visit_notes, outcome_doctype/outcome_name)
└── documents        → Trip Document         (Documents tab: title, kind, file, traveler "Only
                                              For" (blank = everyone), hidden booking_group =
                                              the booking it is on (blank = the whole trip),
                                              booking_label set by the planner)

Travel POI        reusable Point of Interest (Geolocation field feeds the maps)
Travel Settings   Single: per-diem rate rules (Travel Per Diem Rate child), mileage rate,
                  Expense Claim Type mapping, auto-advance + notifications master switches
```

**Shared cost block** (identical on the five cost tables): `estimated_cost`, `cost`, `paid_by` (Company/Employee), `paid_by_traveler` (required+validated when Employee), `billable`, the **Receipt** (fieldname `attachment`; money, never on the views), and the hidden `expense_claim` stamp.

**Plan a Trip fields** (v1.520.0): `leg` (Outbound / Return / During Trip) on Trip Flight and Trip Ground Transport — optional on the form, always set by the page, inferred from the date when blank; hidden `booking_group` on Trip Flight, Trip Accommodation, Trip Ground Transport and Trip Mileage, and on Trip Freight since v1.547.0 (one per shipment, so a bill of lading can belong to it; a shipment typed on the desk form has none, and reads as `row:<name>`, until Plan a Trip next saves it and `merge_freight` gives it one, and one made with the grid's *Duplicate* gives up the id it copied — `TravelTrip._validate_trip_files`). Ground transport gained a **Personal Vehicle** type (riders get a row each at no cost; the driver's miles go to a Trip Mileage row in the same `booking_group`), and a **Company Fleet** row no longer requires a Vehicle — neither `Vehicle` nor `Fleet Vehicle` held a single record on prod, so the requirement made a company truck impossible to enter. A Vehicle Log still needs one.

**Freight and time ranges** (v1.520.0): **Trip Freight** is a carrier shipment — equipment or materials sent to the job — with its tracking / PRO / BOL number, ship-from and deliver-to, a pickup window and a delivery window (from/to datetimes), who on the crew receives it (`traveler`, blank = whole crew, the same visibility rule as a booking), and the shared cost block. It is one row per shipment, not per person, and it is in `COST_TABLES`: rollups, the unclaimed report, the spend-by-category report (its own Freight column) and Expense Claims (as a misc expense — Travel Settings has no freight type) all count it. Our *own* truck's load is not freight: it is the **Hauling** note (`cargo`) on that Ground Transport row. Time ranges: Ground Transport `arrival_datetime` (a drive's end), Trip Accommodation `check_in_time` / `check_out_time`, Trip Agenda `end_time` (its `time` is now labelled Start Time). A stop's **Place** is still a Link to Travel POI; a place picked from Google on the page becomes a POI with its point (`planner.place_to_poi`), reusing one with exactly the same name.

## Money flow

- **Per diem** — per traveler, computed in `validate` from the traveler's own date range: `rate × (days−2) + 2 × rate × first_last_day_pct` (single day = one edge day). Rate comes from Travel Settings by `travel_type`, overridable per traveler. Frozen once claimed (`per_diem_claimed`); later date changes warn instead of silently recomputing.
- **Mileage** — `distance × rate` (settings default, row-overridable). Company fleet never goes here — it's a Ground Transport row + draft **Vehicle Log** (`api.create_vehicle_log`; HRMS validates odometer continuity on submit).
- **Expense Claims** — `api.create_expense_claim(s)` gathers, per traveler: employee-paid cost rows (by `paid_by_traveler`), unclaimed mileage, unclaimed per diem → one draft claim per traveler (extends an existing draft). Header gets `company`, `project`, `custom_travel_trip`; detail rows get real dates/descriptions and `project` when billable. **Throws a configuration error if an Expense Claim Type is unset in Travel Settings** (no silent fallback).
- **Dedupe guard (3 layers):** row-level `expense_claim` stamps + traveler `per_diem_claimed` mark claimed material; `integrations.py` doc_events clear every stamp when a claim is cancelled/deleted, making rows claimable again. Stamps are written with `frappe.db.set_value` (never a full parent save) so a colleague's concurrently open form still saves cleanly.
- **Advances** — `api.create_employee_advance` drafts a native Employee Advance per traveler; status mirrors back onto the traveler row via doc_events.
- **Rollups** on the trip (read-only): estimated/actual, company-paid vs employee-paid splits, per-diem and mileage totals, claimed and advance totals (the last two also refreshed by doc_events).

## Trip views and the money rule

Nik's rule for the trip views (2026-09-26) is **"all but money"**: anyone who can open a trip — the crew, its owner, the travel coordinators — sees every person's bookings, times, confirmation numbers and booking files on the Overview, Crew grid, Side by side and View as, and on `/itinerary?as=`. **Money is for coordinators only** on those views: cost, estimated cost, who paid, billable, per diem, expense claims, advances, rollups and totals, mileage, and the claimed flags. A coordinator is `travel_trip.user_is_travel_coordinator()`: Administrator, System Manager, HR Manager or Travel Coordinator.

It is enforced by what the server sends, never by the page hiding it:

- `api/travel.shape_itinerary` carries no money at all. It is the one shape behind `/itinerary`, the itinerary email and the views, so the three cannot disagree about who is on what. It only ever *gains* keys (the emails read the old ones by name): every booking and shipment has `group` (the checklist's `completeness.group_key`, so a gap lands on its booking — for a shipment too, since v1.547.0 gave it a `booking_group`), and the whole-crew view adds `members` (each row's own person and confirmation number; on a room, whether they are a `guest` and their own `check_in_date`/`check_out_date` when those differ from the room's) and `whole_crew`. Every booking and shipment also carries `documents`, its files this viewer sees, and the answer carries `documents`, every file this viewer sees (see [Trip files](#trip-files)). The one key it ever *lost* is `attachment`: that is the cost row's Receipt, and a receipt is money (v1.547.0; no email or template read it).
- `views.build_trip_views` (behind `api/travel.get_trip_views`) drops the checklist's `cost` gaps for a non-coordinator ("no cost entered" is itself a fact about money), and builds the `money` block — cost and who paid per booking, keyed like the items' `group` (`group_key`, shipments included), and the total — **only** when the caller is a coordinator. For anyone else `money` is `null`. It also passes the trip's files: `documents` (every one, as the whole-crew view lists them) and `people_documents` (`{employee: [file]}`, what each person's `/itinerary` Documents screen lists, which View as shows).
- `api/travel.preview_itinerary_email` returns the address the email would go to only to a coordinator.
- The person asked for (`get_trip_itinerary`'s `as_employee`, the preview's `employee`) is compared against the crew and never looked up. Anyone else gets "That person is not on this trip." and no name. Both arguments are annotated `str`, so v16's whitelist type check refuses a filter dict before it can reach `frappe.db.get_value` as a filter. That combination briefly turned the refusal into a permission-free yes/no question about any Employee field, CTC included.

**What this does not protect.** Every money field on Travel Trip is permlevel 0 and the Employee role has read and write on the trips it can see, so a crew member still sees costs on the trip form, on the existing Plan a Trip steps (the cost fields and the Review step's "Booked so far") and through REST. Those are unchanged. The rule is about the new views, not a lock on money in general; moving money behind a permlevel is a separate decision.

`tests/test_travel_views.py` walks every non-coordinator payload for money keys at any depth and checks that the fixture's sentinel amounts appear nowhere in its JSON. It also runs the real coordinator gate once, unmocked, down to `user_is_travel_coordinator`, and checks that the itinerary email template (which every preview renders) names no money field. Since v1.547.0 its fixture carries receipts, and every payload — a coordinator's included — is checked for their URLs and for an `attachment` or `receipt` key.

## Trip files

**One row per file.** A Trip Document says what the file is (`kind`: Boarding pass, Booking confirmation, Rental agreement, Bill of lading, Site map, Safety plan, Insurance certificate, Job packet, Other), which booking it is on (`booking_group`, the same id as that booking's rows; blank = the whole trip), and who it is for (`traveler`; blank = everyone on the booking, or the whole crew for a file for the whole trip). `booking_label` ("Southwest WN 1422", the hotel, the rental supplier, "Carrier TRACKING") is set by the planner from the booking; the views read the label from the booking as it is now.

**The upload, then the row.** *Attach a file* opens frappe's own uploader (`frappe.ui.FileUploader`, `allow_multiple`), which inserts a **File attached to the Travel Trip** straight away; the page then adds one row per uploaded file (on that card, titled with the file name, of the card's usual kind, for everyone on it — except a flight's, which starts as the flight's one person's, or the first person on it with no boarding pass or ticket of their own yet: `flight_pass_for`) and the row is saved with the next save like any other edit. The uploader is always private (`allow_toggle_private: false`: frappe otherwise offers a *Private* box per file and *Set all public*, and a public File is served to anyone, signed in or not, at `/files/<the name it was uploaded with>`) and takes no web link (`allow_web_link: false`). So it needs a saved trip — a File is attached to a trip by its name — and an unsaved trip's cards say "Save the trip first to attach files". A file attached to a card that is not saved yet names the card's page key (`new:<n>`), and `planner.merge_documents` stores it under the id that booking is given in the same save (`group_map`, built from `prepare_cards` and `merge_freight`, applied after the bookings and freight are merged). An upload keeps going after its dialog closes (the phone's Back closes every dialog, and so do its X and a tap outside it), so a file can land at any moment, and `add_document` puts it where it is not lost: **during a save** it waits for the answer, because that save sent the page before the file was on it and its answer replaces the page's trip (in review, a file landing then vanished, the page read as saved, and the File was left on no list); **while its trip loads again** (Back onto the page from the form) it waits for it; and on a trip **put aside unsaved** it goes on that trip, which *Carry on* brings back with it. Once the cards were redrawn it stays on its booking when that booking already had its id, and is listed with the whole trip's files, with a note, when it did not. Only a trip that is no longer on the page at all gets "no longer open here": the File is attached to it, on its full form, but on no list.

**An upload does not make the page's next save "out of date".** Checked in frappe `version-16`: `handler.upload_file` only inserts the File (`frappe/handler.py` ~129–219); `File.after_insert` → `create_attachment_record` → `add_comment_in_reference_doc` → `Document.add_comment` only inserts an "Attachment" Comment (`file.py` 146–148, 910–926; `document.py` 1743–1764); and `Comment.on_update` → `update_comment_in_doc` returns early for anything but a plain "Comment" (`comment.py`), which in any case writes `_comments` without touching `modified`. This app's File `after_insert` hook (`drive_sync.on_file_attached`) returns early for a Travel Trip. So nothing saves the trip or moves its `modified`, and no extra endpoint was needed.

**Only this trip's files.** `merge_documents` accepts a new or changed file only when a File with that `file_url` is attached to *this* trip (`attached_to_doctype = "Travel Trip"`, `attached_to_name` = the trip), so a pasted URL or another trip's file is refused ("That file is not attached to this trip."), and so is a **public** File, with its own reason ("… is public: anyone with the link can open it …"). The page's uploader cannot make one, but the form's sidebar and the grid's *Attach* use frappe's stock uploader, which can; making those private too is the site's System Setting *Only allow System Managers to upload public files*. It also refuses a file that is one of the trip's Receipts, a person not on the crew (naming the file), a kind that is not an option, and a booking the trip does not have. A file already on the row is not checked again: a File deleted since must not make the whole trip unsaveable. What changed elsewhere since is never a refusal either, because the page sends every file on every save and a refusal would stop every later save of the trip: a saved file whose booking was removed becomes a file for the whole trip (`get_state` already reports it that way), and one still pinned to someone taken off the crew on the form comes off the trip's files (the form checks nothing on the Documents tab), each with a note to the page.

**A receipt is never a trip file, in either order.** frappe v16 reuses a `file_url` when the same content is uploaded again to the same trip (`File.validate_duplicate_entry`), so a PDF put on the trip as paperwork and later as a cost's Receipt — or the other way round — is one URL on both. `TravelTrip._validate_trip_files` refuses such a save on every path, the form's included, naming both rows. And every reader leaves a Trip Document naming a receipt out (`completeness.receipt_urls`): `api/travel._trip_files` (the views, `/itinerary`, View as), `planner._documents_state` (the page, whose next save takes the row off with a note) and the paperwork check, so what the server sends keeps the money rule whatever order the two came in.

**Taking a file off the list leaves the File on the trip.** Removing a row (the ✕ on the page, a removed booking, a person taken off the crew — unticking someone on *Who's going* asks first when they are on a booking or have files of their own, since ticking them again brings neither back) deletes only the Trip Document row. The File stays attached to the trip, in the form's sidebar, and frappe v16 deletes no File when a child row or an Attach value changes. That is deliberate: the page's ✕ is "not one of the trip's files", not "delete this upload", and a file shared by two rows must not vanish from under the other.

**Who sees what — all but money.** Booking files are not money: anyone who can open the trip sees them on the whole-crew views and `/itinerary?as=crew`. One person's view (`/itinerary`, View as) lists the files that are theirs, and the ones for nobody in particular on the whole trip or on a booking they are on (`completeness.document_visible_to`). **That per-person filter is presentation, not access control:** a private file follows frappe's File permission, which defers to read access on the attached trip, so anyone who can read the trip can open any of its files by URL. That fits the rule; do not describe the filter as protection.

**The paperwork check flags, never blocks, and only a booking that is made** (`completeness.document_gaps`, last in `find_gaps`). "The booking is made, so its paperwork should be here": only a booking that already has its confirmation number (a shipment, its tracking number) is asked, so a booking without one gets the confirmation flag and nothing else. Flights are per person — each person with their PNR needs a Boarding pass or Booking confirmation of their own, and `employee_names` names who is still missing one. A file for nobody in particular covers the whole flight only when it can be everyone's: the Booking confirmation (the ticket), or a boarding pass on a flight with one person. A boarding pass for nobody on a shared flight is somebody's and nobody knows whose: counted as everyone's, one person's pass uploaded without saying whose cleared the whole flight and showed on everyone's itinerary as theirs, which is also why the page starts a flight's upload as a person's; a room needs its Booking confirmation, a rental (*Rental/Third Party*) its Rental agreement or confirmation, a shipment its Bill of lading or confirmation. A company truck, a personal car and a taxi come with no paperwork. The gap is `{check: "documents", step, table, group, label, employee_names, kinds}` on the same step as that booking's confirmation gap.

**Paperwork is a separate, quieter tally** (Nik, 2026-09-26). `find_gaps` still returns the paperwork gaps as data, to every consumer (the form's `__onload.trip_gaps`, `get_state`, `build_trip_views` for coordinators and crew alike, since a booking's files are not money), and each carries `check: "documents"` so it can be told apart (`completeness.is_paperwork`). What is never done is *count* them with the rest. Everything that counts "missing" counts `completeness.counted_gaps`: the form's headline, the page's step tab badges, *Mark as booked*'s "N things on the checklist are still missing" (a trip whose only open items are files is booked without a question), a red card, and the Overview's *Still missing* tile. Paperwork is counted on its own, as files (`completeness.files_not_attached`: each person on a flight still without a boarding pass or ticket, one file for any other booking), and shown muted, never in red: a note on the card, the Files step's "10 files not attached yet" list, Review's fifth section *Paperwork* with its own count, a muted *Files not attached* tile on the Overview only when there are some, a muted note on the booking there, and on the form a quiet line under the headline linking to `step=files` — under a green "Trip checklist: nothing missing." when nothing else is open. The Crew grid, Side by side and View as never showed paperwork. `plan_a_trip.js` (`tp_files_not_attached`) and `travel_trip.js` (`files_not_attached`) count the way Python does.

**Why.** The checklist had just been made to stop crying wolf: v1.544.0 (#1133) took the first real trip, TRIP-2026-00001, from six flags to none by no longer asking for what was never needed (six to one by the new rules, and the last cleared by adding the one-night guest to his colleague's room). Every booking on that trip has its number and no file yet, so counted as gaps its paperwork would have put ten straight back, in the same orange as a person with no bed. A paperwork flag is real — the booking is made, so the file should be here — but it is not that kind of problem, and a headline that cannot tell the two apart gets ignored. Existing trips with numbers and no files show the quiet tally from the first load after deploy (`TestTheKaptureTrip`, TRIP-2026-00001 as modeled with its guest added: 0 missing, 10 files not attached).

## Permissions

|---|---|

## Gotchas

- Expense Claims / Advances / Vehicle Logs are **drafts** — HR submits natively.
- Claim generation **refuses to run** until the Expense Claim Types are picked in Travel Settings (they need company accounts, so they are not auto-seeded).
- Per-diem rates seed at **0** (patch `seed_travel_settings`) — finance must set real numbers before per diem produces amounts.
- All travel emails are off until **Travel Settings → Send Travel Notifications** is enabled; the form's "Send Itinerary" button works regardless (explicit user action).
- Deleting a traveler row with a linked claim/advance is blocked; cancel the documents first.
- The daily job also advances **Planning** trips inside their dates to In Progress (crews forget to click Booked); drop "Planning" from the tuple in `tasks.py` to require Booked.
- **A flight or drive with no time yet is stored at midnight**, because a Datetime column cannot hold a date alone. Plan a Trip therefore reads a stored `00:00:00` as "no time given" — a flight genuinely leaving at 12:00 AM shows a blank time on the page (the form shows it correctly).
- **Back and Forward are history entries the page writes itself** (`history.pushState` with the query string, from `window.location.pathname` — frappe v16's `set_route` drops the query, and routing to the page it is on is what broke v1.520.0). A step the user moves to is pushed. **Back is never held up**: a move to an earlier step, or wherever the phone's Back leads, saves quietly and moves whether or not the save went through (the edits stay on the page, marked *Not saved*). An earlier version ran Back through the same blocking save as Next, so one half-finished card — a flight with no airline yet — showed a modal on every press of Back and overwrote each entry it refused, rewriting history into copies of the step you were stuck on. A move **forward** passes the Next button's checks and save; a Forward that is refused goes back (`history.go`) to the entry of the step still showing, leaving the entry asked for in place, and shows its message only once it is back there, because frappe closes any open dialog on every route change (`router.set_history` → `hide_open_dialog`) — which is also why that save is made `silent` and the server's refusal shown afterwards. Every entry the page writes is marked in `history.state` (`tp: {draft, back, pos}`): `pos` is how the page tells Back from Forward and how far to undo; a new trip's `?new=1` entries carry its draft id, so Back onto one after the trip was saved reopens that trip — an unmarked `?new=1` (the list's *+ Add*) is the only kind that starts a blank one. An entry frappe itself pushed for the trip already on screen (the form's *Plan step by step*, a checklist link — v16 pushes the bare path) is rewritten in place to name that trip and step, or Back onto it later, or a reload, would show the list. A trip the list replaces before the server would take it (nobody on it yet, or a refused save) is kept in memory and offered on the list; `beforeunload` still warns while one is unsaved. A trip view is the step's address plus `&view=` (and `&as=`), so it is an entry of its own: `route_view` redraws it on Back/Forward, since `go()` to the step already on screen draws nothing. Opening a view saves quietly first and is never refused — nothing is edited in a view — and a view drawn while that save was refused says the latest changes are not in it (a view has no *Not saved* pill: that lives in the Back/Next bar, and "Back to planning" marks the edit *Not saved* again). The views draw from one `get_trip_views` answer per saved version of the trip (`name@modified`), and an answer for a screen already left is dropped, not kept. A move that lands back on the same view (a refused Forward comes straight back) asks again for an answer it has just dropped, or the view would say "Loading..." for good. The trip on screen is **loaded again** when it is asked for from outside the page and nothing unsaved is on it. That covers frappe's own entries (the form's *Trip views* and *Plan step by step*, a checklist link) and any entry reached back from another page. The trip may have been saved there, and `name@modified` is only the page's copy of the version: the Crew grid used to draw the old cached answer, and View as swapped a person added on the form for the default one. With something unsaved on the page, only the views are asked for again. View as for someone the saved trip does not have still shows the default person, but says so. `scripts/test_wizard_back_forward.mjs` runs the real page against a port of the v16 router (run by `tests/test_travel_planner.py`).
- **The page and the server each list a booking's shared fields** (`TP_SHARED` in `plan_a_trip.js`, `BOOKING_TABLES` in `planner.py`), and likewise a shipment's (`TP_FREIGHT` / `FREIGHT_FIELDS`, `booking_group` last) and a trip file's (`TP_DOCUMENT` / `DOCUMENT_FIELDS`, and `TP_DOC_KINDS` against the Trip Document `kind` options). Add a field to one and not the other and edits to it are dropped with no error; `tests/test_travel_planner.py` fails the build on a mismatch. The page sends a shipment's page key (its stored `booking_group`, `row:<name>` for one typed on the form, `new:<n>` for one added on the page) as its `booking_group`, exactly as a card sends its `group`, so its files can follow it to its id.
- **Removing someone from the crew on the page takes them off every booking** (the server refuses a booking for a non-traveler). Their rows are deleted on save unless they are on an Expense Claim. Since v1.547.0 it also drops the files pinned to them (the File stays attached), hands a shipment they were to receive to the whole crew and one they paid for back to the company, and asks first when they are on a booking or have files of their own (`untick_question`) — before then, removing a shipment's receiver made every later save of the trip fail with "… receives a shipment but is not in the crew".
- **Trip files added on the desk form are checked for one thing only.** The crew, attached-to-this-trip and private checks run in `planner.merge_documents`, i.e. on Plan a Trip saves; the controller refuses only a trip file that is also a cost's Receipt (`_validate_trip_files`). The form limits *Only For* to the crew (`travel_trip.js` `set_query`). A trip file added on the desk form is always a file for the whole trip: its booking cannot be set there (`booking_group` is hidden and read-only) and Plan a Trip cannot move it onto one, so booking paperwork has to be attached from the booking's card on Plan a Trip. The **Trip Documents** table is `no_copy`: the form's *Duplicate* would otherwise copy rows pointing at Files attached to the original trip, which the copy's crew cannot open.
